## 2. Evidence interpretation and readiness vocabulary

Three separate questions apply to every requirement:

- **Mathematical verification (Math):** independently evaluate the stated equation, unit conversion, domain and numerical representation. Correct arithmetic does not establish a correct measurand or diagnosis.
- **Laboratory compatibility (Lab):** verify assay, analyzer/version, units, specimen identity/timing, report fields, analytical uncertainty, reference-profile mapping and preanalytical limitations for the intended laboratory.
- **Clinical validity (Clinical):** establish safe applicability to the population/context, limitations, workflow, wording and clinically reviewed expected outcomes. Recomputed gas bicarbonate is not an independent physiological measurement.

One primary readiness class is assigned per row to identify its immediate review need. Other unresolved layers remain visible; classes are not a maturity scale and none means APPROVED.

| Readiness class | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| EVIDENCE AVAILABLE — NOT APPROVED | Sufficient evidence to begin focused review of the mathematical/conceptual question; professional adoption and applicable local evidence still required. |
| EVIDENCE INCOMPLETE | Required population, method or independent clinical evidence package is absent/incomplete. |
| LOCAL LABORATORY POLICY REQUIRED | Closure depends primarily on the intended laboratory's method/provenance/profile rules and evidence. |
| CLINICAL EXPERT APPROVAL REQUIRED | A clinical applicability, risk/workflow or communication decision needs expert review; source support alone cannot select it. |

C = emergency/critical-care clinical owner with acid–base/nephrology expertise; L = laboratory medicine/clinical biochemistry lead; POCT = point-of-care lead; Ped = pediatric specialist; E = engineering; U = intended-user/usability reviewer. These are required roles, not assigned people. **All professional approval records are unassigned/PENDING.** E can verify arithmetic and conformance, not approve clinical policies. Queue groups Q1–Q7 are defined in section 5.

## 7. Policies software testing alone cannot approve

Software can verify a specified equation, conversion, state transition or boundary. It cannot supply professional decisions about:

- Clinical intended use, population/age/sample eligibility, neonatal exclusion boundaries or transfer of adult evidence to children/VBG.
- Measurand choice, assay equivalence, specimen compatibility, timing windows, preanalytical limitations and local analyzer/field conventions.
- Local reference intervals, diagnostic thresholds, delta baselines or their corrected/uncorrected applicability.
- Albumin coefficient/reference, extrapolation/domain or clinical benefit/limitations in a particular population.
- Significant inconsistency, physiologic plausibility, uncertainty-informed rejection/withholding thresholds or negative/extreme-result clinical action.
- Delta applicability, near-zero/negative denominator clinical safeguards, baseline uncertainty and numerical usefulness.
- Clinically meaningful reporting precision and acceptable uncertainty; binary error allowance is not clinical tolerance.
- Communication safety, missed mixed-process risk, intended-user comprehension, residual-risk acceptance or patient-care readiness.
- Clinical truth of fixture outcomes, applicability of external publications/IFUs to the intended laboratory, professional approvals, implementation authorization or release approval.

Historical passes reported in Phase 4C/F/N remain engineering observations only. No executable suite is run in Phase 4E and no test result is promoted to clinical evidence.
